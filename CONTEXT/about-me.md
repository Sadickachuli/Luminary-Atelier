# about-me.md

Merley is the founder of **Celebrate Sweetly** (corporate gifting and relationship marketing) and **Luminary Atelier** (a new division built around gatherings, experiences, and human connection). She is a creative and a connector, not a professional writer. Meet her there.

## The through-line
Everything she does runs on one belief: **gratitude through thoughtfulness.** Gifting is not a transaction; it is proof that someone was listening. Her signature story is the tiny ceramic frog she included in a new-homeowner gift because the recipient had mentioned, in passing, that she loved tiny frogs. The recipient's thank-you note singled out the frog — because it proved someone heard her. That frog is the whole philosophy in one object.

## What she values
- **Listening before giving.** Do the homework. Know the person — not "they read," but *what* they read. A personal touch is possible even in a fifty-person group gift.
- **Restraint.** Less is more. The right detail beats fluff and filler every time.
- **Sincerity over performance.** She gives because she wants to, never because the calendar says she has to.
- **The relationship as the point.** Both businesses are two halves of one idea: Celebrate Sweetly is the tangible (the object that proves you listened); Luminary Atelier is the intangible (the experience, the connection in the room).

## How she thinks and works
- Persuades with **real stories, not philosophy** — the frog, or the cautionary tales (steaks sent to a vegetarian, alcohol to someone in recovery). Reach for the story.
- **Feels deeply, expresses with restraint.** When genuinely moved, she goes still first, then composes. Not a "this is everything!!" person.
- **Soften and steer, never bulldoze.** Disagrees by asking questions ("have you thought this through?") and offering suggestions, letting people arrive at the answer themselves.
- **Warm and sincere by default.** Humor only with people she knows well enough to know it lands.

## Who she's for / not for
- **For:** people who treat gratitude as a natural part of being, not just doing business.
- **Not for:** anyone who sees appreciation as optional or as an obligation to check off.

## What she wants from an assistant
Write in her real voice, not the corporate "LinkedIn costume." Lead with feeling and with the other person, not with "I'm thrilled to announce." Earn words like *elevated* and *quiet luxury* with concrete detail; never use them as slogans. When in doubt, tell the story.
